## Findings

### 1. [major] Rate normalization depends on number and unit spelling

- **Where:** `pulsern/api/_speak.js:114` · confidence medium
- **Problem:** RATE runs before unit-name and number-word normalization, but recognizes only digits and abbreviated units. Equivalent rate expressions can therefore reach the exact-word checker with different separators. For example, speakable('Give 5 milligrams/minute.') produces 'Give 5 milligrams slash minute.'; canonUnits turns that into 'Give 5 mg / minute.', while the equivalent transcript 'Give 5 mg/minute.' becomes 'Give 5 mg per minute.'. The added tests cover only rate spellings that already match RATE.
- **How it fails:** An accurate reading is transcribed using an abbreviated unit instead of its expanded name, or with number words—for example, 'Respirations twelve breaths/min.' for 'Respirations 12 breaths per minute.'. Under the documented exact-word comparison, the retained slash differs from 'per', so both accurate takes can be rejected and a failed record cached, unnecessarily disabling the checked voice for that passage.
- **Fix:** Normalize equivalent numeric and unit spellings before canonicalizing rate separators, including slashes reconstructed from the word 'slash'. Add regression tests for expanded versus abbreviated units and digit versus number-word rates, while retaining rejection tests for changed values and frequencies.
