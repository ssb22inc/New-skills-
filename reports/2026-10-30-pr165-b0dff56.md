## Findings

### 1. [major] Wave-name normalization rejects capitalization-only transcript differences

- **Where:** `pulsern/api/_speak.js:143` · confidence medium
- **Problem:** The new wave-name regex uses `g`, not `gi`. It converts `T waves` to `Twaves` but leaves `T Waves` as two words. Under the exact-token comparison documented for voiceCheck, capitalization alone therefore becomes a word mismatch. The imported audioCheck implementation was not supplied, so I could not verify this end-to-end.
- **How it fails:** The script is `Hyperkalemia causes peaked T waves.` and the transcriber writes the correctly spoken audio as `Hyperkalemia causes peaked T Waves.` The canonical strings contain `Twaves` versus `T Waves`. If both takes receive that capitalization, the passage is recorded as failed and checked audio becomes unavailable despite an accurate reading.
- **Fix:** Make the wave-name rule case-insensitive and add a regression test requiring voiceCheck to accept `T waves` versus `T Waves`, including a hyphenated mixed-case variant.
