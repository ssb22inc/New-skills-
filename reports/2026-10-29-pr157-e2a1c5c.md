### 2. [major] Concatenating normalized halves does not produce the normalized closed compound

- **Where:** `pulsern/ops/narrate-lib.mjs:290` · confidence high
- **Problem:** The plural normalization is length-dependent, so normalizing each half and concatenating is not equivalent to normalizing the closed spelling. For example, "Follow-ups are important." produces the folded token "followups": the three-letter token "ups" retains its s. The faithful transcript "Followups are important." instead produces "followup", because the complete word loses its final s. audioCheck therefore rejects this legitimate spelling despite the new compound-handling contract. The non-smokers test misses this because "smokers" is already long enough to undergo plural normalization.
- **How it fails:** A narration script containing "follow-ups" or "check-ups" is correctly transcribed as "followups" or "checkups". Both correct takes fail QA, paid recordings are rejected, and recordAll stops the remaining run.
- **Fix:** Map both open and closed representations to a canonical token obtained by normalizing the joined original spelling, rather than concatenating already-normalized halves. Retain the numeric guards and add passing tests for follow-ups/followups and check-ups/checkups, alongside meaningful-corruption rejection tests.
