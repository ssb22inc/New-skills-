## Findings

### 1. [blocker] A potassium replacement dose is parsed as severe hyperkalemia

- **Where:** `pulsern/src/diagrams/match.js:54` · confidence high
- **Problem:** The expanded two-digit matcher accepts a number after "potassium" without requiring laboratory context or a concentration unit. Because the laboratory-unit suffix is optional, "potassium 10 mEq" is accepted as a serum potassium of 10 mEq/L. The new extraction range admits that value.
- **How it fails:** For "A client with hypokalemia is prescribed potassium 10 mEq by mouth daily. Which instruction should the nurse give?", extractPotassium returns {"k":10}. The proposed patient-specific diagram consequently describes extreme, severe hyperkalemia instead of remaining concept-only. A separate pairing reviewer would have to catch this incorrect proposal.
- **Fix:** Distinguish replacement doses from laboratory results before returning patient parameters. Reject standalone dose units such as mEq without /L, and require an explicit result unit or unambiguous laboratory context. Add a regression test for an oral 10 mEq prescription alongside the existing true 10.0 mEq/L result test.

### 2. [blocker] The route guard does not recognize normal dotted I.V. notation

- **Where:** `pulsern/src/diagrams/match.js:116` · confidence high
- **Problem:** The I\.V\. alternative ends in a period but is followed by the shared trailing word boundary. A period followed by whitespace or end-of-string is not a word boundary, so ordinary "I.V." notation escapes the non-subcutaneous route guard.
- **How it fails:** extractInsulin("Regular U-100 insulin was given I.V. at 0700.") returns {"type":"short","givenAt":"07:00"} instead of null. If that proposal is accepted, the worked diagram presents a 09:00–11:00 subcutaneous peak window for an intravenous dose, teaching the wrong action timing.
- **Fix:** Use punctuation-aware boundaries for dotted route abbreviations, or normalize route notation before testing it. Add regression tests for "I.V. at 0700" and dotted notation at the end of a sentence, ensuring both remain concept-only.
