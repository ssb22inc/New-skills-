### 1. [major] First-time NCLEX appointment timing changed from "within 30 days" to "30 days after"

- **Where:** `pulsern/ops/learn-logistics.mjs:133` · confidence medium
- **Problem:** The public schedule-reschedule guide previously said first-time candidates "will be offered an appointment within 30 days of the scheduling request"; after the change it says "30 days after the scheduling request". Those are different rules: "within 30 days" permits an offer earlier than day 30, while "30 days after" implies the candidate will not be offered a seat before day 30. If the cited NCSBN scheduling page uses "within 30 days" (the old wording mirrors that), this is false exam-policy content reaching students.
- **How it fails:** A nursing student reads the page and postpones scheduling/assumes no appointment can exist before 30 days, based on a narrowed paraphrase of the official rule.

### 2. [major] Type pages now assert exact NCSBN scoring-rule mappings that the linked sources do not visibly support

- **Where:** `pulsern/ops/learn-types.mjs:20` · confidence low
- **Problem:** The change replaces the earlier safe language—e.g. "NCSBN states generally that items with more than one key can receive partial credit under plus/minus, zero/one or rationale scoring… Do not assume…"—with definitive mappings: bow-tie is zero/one, SATA is plus/minus because the count is unstated, matrix is zero/one per row unless multiple columns are selectable, cloze blanks are zero/one or rationale, and highlight is plus/minus or zero/one depending on the instruction. The linked evidence in these pages/content-provenance is NCSBN Test Plan/FAQ/NGN pages whose recorded locators describe partial-credit methods, not an item-type-by-item-type scoring assignment. If the Test Plan does not actually enumerate these mappings, all five public pages now teach unsupported exam-scoring facts to students, and the updated seo-guardian tests assert those unsupported strings, so the suite passes for the wrong reason.
- **How it fails:** A student reads the SATA/bow-tie/cloze/matrix/highlight guides and adopts a scoring strategy (e.g., "wrong SATA choices always subtract") that the official sources do not establish.
- **Fix:** Cite the exact official document containing the item-type-to-scoring-rule table, or restore the qualified statement that NCSBN identifies three partial-credit methods without a public per-item rule.
