## Findings

### 1. [blocker] Image metadata can pass through without being reviewed

- **Where:** `pulsern/ops/astra-review.mjs:256` · confidence high
- **Problem:** The PNG validator accepts arbitrary chunks with valid lengths and CRCs, including tEXt, iTXt and eXIf. The WebP validator similarly accepts metadata chunks. collectChanges then represents those files only as hashes and image attachments. A vision rendering does not expose their metadata, so container validation does not establish the claimed lossless review.
- **How it fails:** A commit adds a service-role key or patient identifier to a valid text/metadata chunk in a public PNG and recomputes its CRC. The validator accepts it, while the before and after pictures look identical to the reviewer. The sensitive bytes can consequently be published without ever appearing in the review's textual input.
- **Fix:** Reject unreviewed ancillary chunks, or extract their contents into a separately reviewed representation and fail closed on unsupported metadata. Apply the same policy to WebP. Add an end-to-end test changing only a valid metadata chunk and prove that its contents reach the reviewer or the change is rejected.

### 2. [blocker] Treating every A as an article hides clinical omissions

- **Where:** `pulsern/ops/narrate-lib.mjs:157` · confidence high
- **Problem:** speechTokens lowercases everything and removes every token equal to "a". This also removes clinically meaningful letters, such as the serotype in "hepatitis A". The similarity threshold does not rescue the check because dropping one letter from a full narration remains well above 0.92.
- **How it fails:** audioCheck("Hepatitis A spreads by the fecal oral route. Teach careful hand hygiene to reduce transmission.", "Hepatitis spreads by the fecal oral route. Teach careful hand hygiene to reduce transmission.") passes: the token arrays become identical and similarity remains high. The accepted recording incorrectly generalizes hepatitis A's transmission route to hepatitis overall.
- **Fix:** Do not discard articles indiscriminately where they can be clinical identifiers. Preserve meaningful letters and abbreviations, failing closed when their role is ambiguous. Add positive and omission tests for hepatitis A, vitamin A and blood-group A, and bump QA_VERSION so clips accepted by the weaker normalization are rechecked.

### 3. [blocker] An out-of-range compensatory value still gets a definitive fully compensated label

- **Where:** `pulsern/src/diagrams/abg.jsx:87` · confidence high
- **Problem:** The normal-pH branch always returns compensation: "full" and states "fully compensated", including when compensationLimit establishes that the opposing abnormality exceeds expected compensation. Appending "a mixed disorder is possible" does not make the preceding definitive interpretation correct. The new tests explicitly preserve this contradictory result.
- **How it fails:** For pH 7.38, PaCO₂ 20 and HCO₃⁻ 11.4, Winter's expected PaCO₂ is approximately 23.1–27.1. The lower measured PaCO₂ suggests an additional respiratory alkalosis, not simply fully compensated metabolic acidosis. Nevertheless, the diagram and worked caption lead with "Metabolic acidosis, fully compensated".
- **Fix:** When expected-compensation checking fails, do not assert a single fully or partially compensated disorder. Return an appropriately qualified possible-mixed-disorder interpretation and make the chip and caption consistent with it. If retaining ROME for exam teaching, explicitly identify it as a preliminary pattern rather than the final interpretation. Update the tests that currently require compensation: "full" despite an out-of-range result.
