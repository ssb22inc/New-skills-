## Assessment

Do not ship yet. The INR correction is appropriate, but two reconciliation defects can persist incorrect ability and streak state. The existing tests do not cover these mixed-client and delayed-upload scenarios. I reviewed the supplied changes; I did not execute the test suite.

## Findings

### 1. [blocker] Conflict reconciliation drops ability updates from legacy-client answers

- **Where:** `pulsern/src/guest.js` · confidence high
- **Problem:** replayUnseen skips every answer without an attempt ID. Nevertheless, when any new attempt exists, reconcileProgress replaces the merged ability with replayed.ability. Answers written by the previous app version therefore survive in the merged log but lose their ability updates. Migration 022 explicitly permits those older clients to keep writing.
- **How it fails:** An updated tab loads baseline L. A still-open older tab answers a Pharmacology question and saves L+B without an attempt ID. The updated tab answers a Management of Care question and encounters a revision conflict. Reconciliation preserves both B and the local answer in the log, but rebuilds ability from L plus only the local answer. Pharmacology's n and theta revert to their baseline values even though B remains counted in the answer history used by readiness.
- **Fix:** Handle newly added legacy occurrences explicitly instead of replacing ability with an attempt-only replay. Preserve their scored ability contribution, or establish stable identity and sufficient replay metadata before reconstructing ability. If mixed-version writes cannot be merged safely, require the older client to reload rather than silently accepting inconsistent progress. Add a regression using a server-side new answer without attempt and a local new answer with attempt, asserting both categories' ability updates and readiness inputs.

### 2. [blocker] Replaying an older upload does not rewind the streak before rebuilding it

- **Where:** `pulsern/src/guest.js` · confidence high
- **Problem:** replayOrdered removes and undoes the affected answer tail, but initializes its reconstruction with blankScore(base), which retains base.streak after that tail was already applied. Earlier activity is then ignored by advanceStreak because its date precedes lastDay. The later special case preserves the server streak only when the local streak is unchanged, so it does not protect a student who has continued studying.
- **How it fails:** The baseline contains Tuesday's first answer and a one-day streak. Another device uploads a delayed Monday answer, producing server history Monday–Tuesday and a two-day streak. Before learning about that upload, the local device answers on Wednesday, also reaching a two-day streak from its Tuesday baseline. Merging server [Monday, Tuesday] with local [Tuesday, Wednesday] replays from the already-counted Tuesday streak: Monday is ignored, Tuesday is ignored, and Wednesday increments it to two. The saved history contains three consecutive study days, but streak.count and the longest-streak best remain two.
- **Fix:** Reconstruct streak and protection consumption from a checkpoint before the replayed tail, including dated question and card activity, rather than retaining the post-tail streak. Do not rely on choosing the server streak only for setting-only local changes. Add a regression for baseline Tuesday, delayed remote Monday, and newly answered local Wednesday; the merged streak must be three.
