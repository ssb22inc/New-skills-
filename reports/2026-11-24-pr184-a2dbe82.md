## Findings

### 1. [blocker] Legacy-client saves discard progress while making answer history inconsistent

- **Where:** `pulsern/supabase/migrations/022_progress_revision_guard.sql:26` · confidence high
- **Problem:** Once revision is positive, a legacy save replaces its entire incoming blob with old.blob and appends selected log entries only. Incoming ability, XP, streak, flashcard schedules, exam results, and settings are discarded. Newly appended answers are nevertheless treated as already loaded history on the next app launch, so reconciliation does not subsequently apply their missing ability updates.
- **How it fails:** A student leaves the previous app version open on a phone, opens this version on another device, then studies on the phone. Phone flashcard grades disappear entirely; new question answers may appear in history without their ability, XP, or review-schedule updates. Repeated answers can also disappear because the occurrence comparison uses only id, correctness, and an optional day.
- **Fix:** Replace the log-only compatibility merge with a migration strategy that preserves the semantic progress from older clients, or ensure older clients reload before accepting further study. Add a database-level regression test that executes the trigger with an existing positive revision and verifies question updates, repeated attempts, flashcard grades, and exam results—not just the revision helper or SQL text.

### 2. [blocker] Deleting progress and restarting revisions permits a pre-reset write to overwrite the reset

- **Where:** `pulsern/src/App.jsx` · confidence medium
- **Problem:** resetAll deletes the row, and store.set recreates it at revision 1. A pre-reset UPDATE also conditioned on revision 1 can therefore match the newly created row. The client epoch prevents accepting its response locally, but does not protect the database from that late write.
- **How it fails:** An UPDATE based on revision 1 is delayed before database execution. Reset deletes the original row and autosave inserts the reset blob at revision 1. If the old server request subsequently executes despite the client abort, its revision predicate succeeds and restores pre-reset progress over the new row. The browser regression test does not exercise this: its stub applies the write before waiting on releaseAfter, delaying only the acknowledgement. I could not verify any deployment guarantee that aborting fetch cancels all queued server work.
- **Fix:** Implement reset as an atomic server operation that retains a monotonically increasing revision or changes a persisted generation checked by every write. Do not reset the concurrency token by deleting and reinserting. Test a delayed pre-reset database mutation applied after the reset, independently of client-side abort handling.

### 3. [major] Cover reconciliation charges gaps that merged study history has already filled

- **Where:** `pulsern/src/guest.js` · confidence high
- **Problem:** mergeRewards calculates cover consumption from the two endpoint balances and unions their protected days, rather than using the reconciled chronological activity. A cover spent by one device is never refunded when the other device supplies the study day that made that cover unnecessary.
- **How it fails:** Both devices load Friday progress with a ready shield and one cover. One records Monday after missing Saturday and Sunday, spending the shield and cover. The other records Saturday and saves later. The merged Saturday-to-Monday history needs only the shield for Sunday, and the replay computes that correctly, but mergeRewards still subtracts Monday's independently consumed cover and returns zero instead of one. It also retains Saturday as a protected day even though the student studied then.
- **Fix:** Reconcile cover consumption and protected days from the merged activity timeline, with cover grants deduplicated separately, instead of summing consumption inferred from endpoint snapshots. Add this Friday/Saturday/Monday two-device regression and assert the streak, remaining cover balance, and protected-day list.
