## Assessment

Not ready to merge. The device fallback ignores server content-validation refusals, and its completion/error handling can silently omit passages or leave speech running after playback has moved on. The new request limiter also has an unbounded-wait failure. Static review only; tests were not executed.

## Findings

### 1. [blocker] Honor bank-validation refusals before speaking on the device

- **Where:** `pulsern/src/read-aloud.jsx:441` · confidence high
- **Problem:** Every outcome without an audio URL reaches readOnDevice(), which checks only voiceable(text). Explicit server refusals such as 404 (item unavailable) and 409 (text does not match the current bank item) therefore still cause the rejected text to be spoken. The modified refusal tests force noDeviceVoice=true, so they do not exercise this normal-device path.
- **How it fails:** A student leaves a question open while an RN corrects its clinically erroneous stem or rejects the item. Listen receives 409 or 404 from the server, but the device reads the old stem anyway. The server's approval and exact-text checks no longer control what reaches the student through audio.
- **Fix:** Distinguish content/access refusals from audio-availability failures. Never device-speak text rejected by bank validation. For fallback before a response arrives, obtain authorization bound to the exact text separately rather than treating an unanswered request as permission. Add normal-device tests for rejected and mismatched items.

### 2. [major] Do not silently skip a failed device-speech chunk

- **Where:** `pulsern/src/read-aloud.jsx:340` · confidence high
- **Problem:** spoke is shared across the entire passage. Once any chunk starts, a non-cancellation error in any later chunk calls next(), even if that later chunk never started. The passage eventually resolves true, so readOnDevice() provides no failure indication or screen-reading fallback for the omitted text.
- **How it fails:** The first sentence finishes, the second utterance reports synthesis-failed before starting, and the third sentence succeeds. The student hears sentences one and three while the bar continues to report Device voice; sentence two is silently omitted.
- **Fix:** Treat a non-cancellation utterance error as a failed reading, or retry that specific chunk with a bounded policy. Do not advance as though it completed. Add a multi-chunk test where a later utterance fails before starting and another where it fails after starting.

### 3. [major] Cancel timed-out utterances before finishing or advancing

- **Where:** `pulsern/src/read-aloud.jsx:343` · confidence high
- **Problem:** The watchdog calls next() without cancelling the current utterance or invalidating its callbacks. For the final chunk, next() resolves the passage successfully and removes its abort listener even though the synthesis engine can still have that utterance pending or speaking. Late onend callbacks can also advance a multi-chunk reading a second time.
- **How it fails:** A short utterance remains pending beyond its seven-second watchdog. The hook advances to the next AI clip, then the delayed device utterance starts over it. Pressing Stop after the watchdog cannot cancel that old utterance because finish() already removed its abort listener.
- **Fix:** On timeout, cancel the outstanding synthesis and invalidate its callbacks before resolving or advancing. Do not manufacture a successful start when none occurred. Extend the harness to track pending/speaking utterances and verify that delayed events cannot produce speech after timeout, Stop, or an item change.

### 4. [major] Bound requests holding the shared voice-client slots

- **Where:** `pulsern/src/read-aloud.jsx:108` · confidence high
- **Problem:** The six-slot limiter releases a slot only when token acquisition, fetch, and response parsing settle. None has an application deadline, and changing items does not cancel those requests. The playback timeout only stops waiting for audio; it does not release a request slot.
- **How it fails:** Six requests from a previous item remain pending during a stalled connection. Every request for subsequent items waits behind them without reaching /api/ai, including retries. AI playback remains unavailable until those abandoned requests settle or the page reloads, even if new requests could otherwise succeed.
- **Fix:** Give slot-holding operations a bounded lifetime and abort stalled fetches, or cancel obsolete requests when their item is abandoned. Ensure every timeout/cancellation releases its slot. Test six unresolved requests followed by navigation to a new item and verify that the new item can eventually issue requests.
