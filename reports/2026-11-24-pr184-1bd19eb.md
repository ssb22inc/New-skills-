## Assessment

Request changes. The new handoff and recovery paths can overwrite or discard saved progress, retain authenticated study history after sign-out, and produce readiness estimates that ignore imported answers. Legacy identity handling also misroutes ambiguous history. Findings are based on the supplied source and images; I did not execute the build or tests.

## Findings

### 1. [blocker] Pending recovery replaces newer cloud progress without reconciliation

- **Where:** `pulsern/src/App.jsx:608` · confidence high
- **Problem:** After successfully reading the server row, loadProgress replaces the entire migrated blob with the pending local payload. The pending record has no server revision, updated_at comparison, or expiry. Recovery can therefore overwrite newer answers, XP, streaks, exam results, and flashcard schedules already saved elsewhere.
- **How it fails:** A student's save times out during sign-out, leaving a pending snapshot on browser A. They subsequently study on device B. When they return to browser A, its older snapshot replaces the successfully read cloud state and autosave writes it back, deleting device B's progress.
- **Fix:** Record the server revision on which a pending snapshot was based. Reconcile against the current row before writing, or present a recoverable conflict instead of replacing the cloud blob. Add a regression covering newer cloud progress, including an intentional reset, after the pending snapshot was created.

### 2. [blocker] Another account's successful save deletes pending recovery

- **Where:** `pulsern/src/App.jsx:668` · confidence high
- **Problem:** Every successful autosave calls clearPendingProgress unconditionally. Although readPendingProgress refuses a pending record belonging to another known account, the subsequent save still deletes that other account's recovery copy.
- **How it fails:** Account A signs out after a timed-out save whose request ultimately fails. Account B signs in on the shared browser. B correctly does not restore A's pending payload, but B's initial autosave removes it. A's unsynced answers are then permanently lost.
- **Fix:** Make pending cleanup conditional on the exact account and pending payload successfully recovered by that save. Use an account-scoped key or a compare-and-delete operation so unrelated or newer recovery records survive. Test A's pending recovery across B's load and autosave.

### 3. [blocker] Timed-out sign-out leaves plaintext authenticated history on the device

- **Where:** `pulsern/src/progress-load.js:65` · confidence high
- **Problem:** stashPendingProgress stores the complete authenticated progress blob and user ID in localStorage. The sign-out paths remove the guest and legacy backup keys but deliberately retain this new key, with no expiry. This recreates the shared-device history exposure described in this file's legacy-backup warning under a different key.
- **How it fails:** An upsert takes longer than three seconds and the student signs out. Their full answer history, custom questions, XP, and other saved progress remain readable in pulsern-pending-progress-v1 while signed out and after another person starts using the browser.
- **Fix:** Do not silently retain plaintext authenticated history after logout. Use server-side recovery or an explicitly consented recovery mechanism whose contents cannot be opened without authenticating the owning account. Extend the sign-out privacy test to inspect every progress-bearing storage key, not only the legacy backup key.

### 4. [blocker] Importing trial answers onto history changes readiness evidence but not ability

- **Where:** `pulsern/src/guest.js:365` · confidence high
- **Problem:** importTrialOntoHistory appends imported outcomes to log while leaving ability unchanged. readinessFrom uses log.length for its 12-answer minimum and confidence band, but derives its estimate from ability. The imported answers therefore increase claimed evidence without affecting the ability estimate, even when every imported answer is wrong. The current history-import test explicitly asserts unchanged ability rather than detecting this inconsistency.
- **How it fails:** An account has seven recorded answers and then accepts five distinct incorrect trial answers. Its log reaches 12, enabling a readiness estimate, but its ability still reflects only the original seven answers. For an established account, the same import narrows the confidence band while ignoring the additional misses.
- **Fix:** Incorporate accepted trial outcomes into the ability/evidence calculation without overwriting existing history, using a defined deterministic import policy. Alternatively, explicitly exclude them from readiness evidence and correct the confirmation copy. Test the resulting readiness and category ability, not just preservation of the old ability object.

### 5. [major] Unresolved legacy history is projected onto unrelated questions

- **Where:** `pulsern/src/question-review.js:84` · confidence high
- **Problem:** settledResult assigns an unresolved legacy row's outcome to any catalog question sharing its raw numeric ID, without checking source or category. unseenQuestions then hides that question, and reviewPools treats an unresolved wrong outcome as its miss. The identity-matrix test titled "does not treat an unclassified case row as the bank question" actually asserts this incorrect mapping.
- **How it fails:** A legacy sepsis step logged as 101 collides with an unrelated bank question 101. After classification leaves the row unresolved, a correct case answer makes the never-answered bank question unavailable in both New questions and Review Misses. A wrong case answer instead places the unrelated bank question in Review Misses.
- **Fix:** Do not transfer an unresolved outcome to every matching raw ID. Resolve provenance using sufficient catalog metadata where possible, and otherwise preserve the uncertainty without marking unrelated questions answered or missed. Add tests for both correct and incorrect case/bank collisions that assert the unrelated bank question's actual history remains untouched.
