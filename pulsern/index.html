Retrieval practice

You learn by pulling the answer out of memory. PulseRN does not open a session with a lecture. You commit to an answer, then read the rationale. Flashcards ask you to recall before the answer is shown. That is the same loop on the Today round, in practice, and on the cards.

Spaced repetition

What you miss comes back on a real calendar date, right before you would forget it, so it is there on exam day and not gone after a cram. Cards you know move further out. Cards you miss stay close. The schedule is the study plan, not a pile of cards to reread.

Adaptive practice

PulseRN finds weak spots across the eight NCSBN client-needs categories and works them until they are strengths. Question difficulty follows your answers inside the app. That working estimate guides the next item. It is not an official NCLEX score.

A plan for each day

The Today round is one focused session: due flashcards, then adaptive questions, with the next action in view. Set an exam date and the weekly plan is built around it, so you always know what to do next. The lab reference opens over the question, so a value lookup stays on the item.

Visual study cards

Six concepts are drawn so they click: ABG interpretation, potassium and the ECG changes that go with it, IV fluid tonicity, insulin onset and peak, isolation precautions, and pressure-injury stages. They appear with the questions that use them.

The study library

The library is 10,000+ practice questions, 500+ case studies, and 1,100+ flashcards, with dosage calculation and an AI tutor when you want another explanation. Case studies follow the NCSBN clinical judgment measurement model. Practice items are original. PulseRN does not use, reproduce, or claim access to live NCLEX questions.

Try the 20-question free NCLEX-RN practice test hub, or open a five-question set for pharmacology, prioritization, dosage calculations, or NGN bow-tie items. Each public set shows the answer, the rationale, and the sources it was written against.

Readiness is an estimate

Paid plans include a stated number of standardized 85-question readiness self-assessments. A form is never repeated for the same account. The result is an estimate of your work inside PulseRN. It shows what to practice next. It does not predict or guarantee an NCLEX result.
