Why I built it

I'm a hospital RN, and I remember studying for the NCLEX between shifts. The tools I had were either a giant question bank or a lecture, and neither was built around how memory works.

PulseRN is for the nursing student fitting study around clinicals, work, and the rest of life. Every session starts with a question. What you miss comes back right before you'd forget it, and weak areas get extra reps until they aren't weak anymore.

How content is checked

Practice questions, case studies, flashcards, and readiness items are built around NCSBN's published test plan. Each one passes automated quality checks before it goes live, covering the keyed answer, the distractors, the rationale, and any dosage math. AI assists with drafting items and with on-demand tutoring.

I own the product and can correct or remove any item. Report a problem under a question, and the report is saved for follow-up. Public study guides are a separate set: I review each one against its sources, and the review record is tied to the exact text of the page. The editorial policy has the details.

How scoring works

Readiness self-assessments are graded more strictly than the NCLEX: a multi-part question counts only when every part is right, whereas the NCLEX can give partial credit. Practice questions use NCSBN-style partial credit. Your readiness score is an estimate built from your own answers, explained on the methodology page.

Ready to register for the NCLEX?

Registration takes three steps: apply for licensure with your state board of nursing, register with Pearson VUE, then schedule your exam when your Authorization to Test (ATT) arrives. The official sources are nclex.com, NCSBN's exam site, and Pearson VUE, which handles registration, fees, and scheduling. The registration guide walks through each step.
