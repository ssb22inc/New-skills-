## Findings

### 1. [major · clinical] Intermediate compensation can be acute-on-chronic, not just subacute or mixed

- **Where:** abg.jsx: courseText(), cautionFor(), compensationLimit(); clinical claim 8 · confidence high
- **Problem:** When bicarbonate falls between the acute and chronic estimates, the output offers only 'subacute, or a mixed disorder.' A common alternative is acute-on-chronic respiratory disease. For example, a client with baseline PaCO₂ 60 and HCO₃⁻ 32 who acutely rises to PaCO₂ 80 can have HCO₃⁻ about 34 and pH about 7.25 without an additional metabolic disorder. This input passes the tool's checks but receives the incomplete subacute-or-mixed explanation. The acute-only/chronic-only cautions also need to distinguish the duration of the current PaCO₂ elevation from a history of chronic respiratory disease.
- **Fix:** Explicitly include acute-on-chronic respiratory disorders among the time-course possibilities. Explain that prior gases and the chronology of the PaCO₂ change are needed before attributing a mismatch to an added metabolic process. Do not present the acute and chronic estimates as exhaustive time-course categories.

### 2. [minor · pedagogy] The learner is not told how to choose a primary disorder when pH is normal

- **Where:** Steps 'rome', 'compensation', and 'limits'; images 5–7 and 13–15 · confidence high
- **Problem:** 'Find the value that matches the pH' works for acidemia or alkalemia, but supplies no actionable branch when pH is 7.35–7.45 and both other values are abnormal. The interpreter tests plausible primary disorders against their expected compensation; the explainer never tells the student to do that. Merely listing the formulas after labeling the pattern 'fully compensated' leaves the central selection step unstated.
- **Fix:** Add a normal-pH instruction: assess the abnormal PaCO₂ and HCO₃⁻, test the plausible primary disorders against expected compensation, and retain alternatives when more than one fits. State that normal pH does not by itself identify the primary process.
