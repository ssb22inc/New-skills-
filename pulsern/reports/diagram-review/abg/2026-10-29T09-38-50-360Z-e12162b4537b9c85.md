## Findings

### 1. [major · pedagogy] “Opposite directions” confuses numerical movement with opposing effects on pH

- **Where:** Images 1, 6, 9, 14; step "compensation" caption/narration; CompTile "Partial" and "Full" · confidence high
- **Problem:** “Both values out of range in opposite directions” reads as one value high and the other low. In ordinary compensation, PaCO₂ and HCO₃⁻ generally move in the same numerical direction; their effects on pH oppose each other. For example, pH 7.37 / PaCO₂ 50 / HCO₃⁻ 28 is compensation-compatible and the interpreter labels it fully compensated, although both values are elevated. The tile and caption can therefore teach students to reverse the compensation check.
- **Fix:** Use “both values abnormal, with opposing effects on pH.” Replace every unqualified “opposite way/directions” in the compensation summary with explicit wording about effects on pH. State that PaCO₂ and HCO₃⁻ generally rise together or fall together during compensation.

### 2. [major · pedagogy] Respiratory compensation calculations omit the HCO₃⁻ baseline

- **Where:** Images 1, 7, 9, 15; RULES respiratory rows; step "limits" caption and narration · confidence high
- **Problem:** The student-facing rules give bicarbonate changes per 10 mmHg but never say to start from HCO₃⁻ 24 mEq/L. The picture supplies the PaCO₂ baseline of 40, while the caption and narration omit that baseline too. Students are told to calculate an expected compensation range without being given all the inputs. Choosing any point within the displayed normal bicarbonate range changes the result enough to affect the ±2 comparison.
- **Fix:** Show and narrate both baselines explicitly: start from PaCO₂ 40 mmHg and HCO₃⁻ 24 mEq/L. Prefer complete respiratory equations, including the acute/chronic coefficients and ±2 tolerance, rather than change-only rules.
