## Findings

### 1. [major · pedagogy] Respiratory equations do not clearly scope the coefficient or sign

- **Where:** Images 1, 7, 9 and 15; expected-compensation panel; abg.jsx RULES respiratory equations · confidence high
- **Problem:** “24 − 2 (acute) or 5 (chronic) × …” does not unambiguously apply the subtraction and multiplication to both alternatives. The respiratory-acidosis equation has the same grouping defect. Students copying these as standalone equations can calculate the wrong bicarbonate. For example, at PaCO₂ 20, the intended respiratory-alkalosis estimates are 20 acute and 14 chronic.
- **Fix:** Write HCO₃⁻ ≈ 24 + k × ((PaCO₂ − 40) / 10), with k = 1 acute or 4 chronic; and HCO₃⁻ ≈ 24 − k × ((40 − PaCO₂) / 10), with k = 2 acute or 5 chronic. Retain ±2 and the units.

### 2. [major · consistency] Following the narration can disagree with the computed verdict

- **Where:** Step 'limits' caption and narration versus images 7 and 15 and compensationLimit('metabolic', 'alkalosis') · confidence high
- **Problem:** The caption and narration teach only PaCO₂ ≈ 0.7 × HCO₃⁻ + 21 ±2, but the interpreter accepts either of two formulas. This changes actual decisions. With pH 7.50, PaCO₂ 53 and HCO₃⁻ 40, the narrated rule gives 47–51 mmHg, placing 53 outside expected compensation. The interpreter accepts 47–53.2 and returns partially compensated metabolic alkalosis without a mixed-disorder caution. These values are internally consistent.
- **Fix:** Make the caption and recording acknowledge both accepted formulas and their differing estimates, or standardize the displayed teaching and interpreter on one sourced convention. Add this value set as a cross-modal regression test.
