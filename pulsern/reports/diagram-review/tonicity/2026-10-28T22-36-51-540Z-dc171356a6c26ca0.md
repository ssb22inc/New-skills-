## Assessment

Do not publish. The particle drawings misrepresent concentration, the explanation conflates osmolarity with tonicity, and the safety instruction is unjustifiably absolute. Phone readability and a shared AI-labeling branch also fail the stated rules.

## Findings

### 1. [blocker · clinical] 'Never' turns an avoidance principle into an absolute contraindication

- **Where:** Images 1, 7, 8, 14; step 'safety'; safety box and narration · confidence high
- **Problem:** The facts say hypotonic fluids are 'avoided,' but the picture and narration escalate this to 'never' for any stroke or head injury. Those diagnoses do not alone establish current cerebral edema or override every concurrent indication. For example, post-injury diabetes insipidus with hypernatremia may require carefully managed free-water replacement. The imperative also reads as an unconditional treatment instruction rather than exam-prep education.
- **Fix:** Use consistent, qualified exam-prep wording: 'Hypotonic fluids can worsen cerebral edema and are generally avoided with increased ICP or acute brain injury; fluid selection depends on the clinical context.' Remove 'never' throughout.

### 2. [blocker · clinical] Osmolarity is being taught as tonicity

- **Where:** All images: top rule; facts 1 and 5; steps 'hypotonic', 'hypertonic', 'bags', and 'dextrose' · confidence high
- **Problem:** Cell-volume effects depend on the concentration of effective, nonpenetrating solutes—not all dissolved particles indiscriminately. The diagram then calls its dextrose bag classification 'tonicity' and associates those columns with cellular effects. Conventional NCLEX bag classifications do not establish sustained physiological tonicity. Glucose uptake and metabolism also are not universally rapid, particularly with hyperglycemia or impaired insulin action.
- **Fix:** Define tonicity using effective/nonpenetrating solutes. Clearly distinguish conventional in-bag osmolarity classifications from physiological effects after glucose uptake/metabolism. Keep the familiar bag lists, but do not imply that every listed hyperosmolar dextrose fluid produces the same sustained cell-shrinking effect as 3% saline. Qualify 'used up fast' rather than making clearance unconditional.

### 3. [blocker · visual] Particle counts do not depict the claimed concentrations

- **Where:** Images 1–4 and 8–11: particle fields; tonicity.jsx particles() and Panel() · confidence high
- **Problem:** The code explicitly treats particle counts as the concept. It draws five particles inside each cell and 7, 2, or 14 outside, despite radically different represented compartment sizes. In the isotonic panel, the intracellular dots are visibly much more concentrated than the extracellular dots. The hypertonic panel likewise does not depict a higher extracellular density across the represented solution. Counting more dots in a larger space is not a concentration comparison.
- **Fix:** Show equal-volume sampling regions with equal solute density for isotonic fluid, lower extracellular density for hypotonic fluid, and higher extracellular density for hypertonic fluid. Separate the initial concentration comparison from the final swollen/shrunken cell shapes so the driving gradient and endpoint are not conflated.

### 4. [major · accessibility] Most diagram text is too small at phone width

- **Where:** All images, especially inline images 1 and 8; fluid lists, panel notes, legend, dextrose and safety text · confidence high
- **Problem:** Essential content uses 9.5–10.5-unit SVG text. Scaling the 360-unit canvas into a padded phone container reduces much of it to approximately 8–10 CSS pixels. The larger explainer captions do not make the static rationale diagram readable.
- **Fix:** Reflow the diagram for narrow screens instead of shrinking the entire layout. Use larger text and stacked sections, or move detailed fluid lists and safety text into responsive HTML. Verify readability at the actual minimum supported phone width.

### 5. [major · accessibility] Step dimming makes retained context unreadable

- **Where:** Images 2–7 and 9–14: out-of-focus text; kit.jsx G() · confidence high
- **Problem:** Whole-group opacity of 0.18 puts retained text far below the required 4.5:1 contrast in both themes. Unfocused fluid lists are nested inside another unfocused group, so their effective opacity becomes 0.18 × 0.18 = 0.0324. They are effectively invisible, not usable context.
- **Fix:** Preserve compliant text contrast and indicate focus with borders, weight, or another non-opacity treatment. If inactive details should disappear, remove them deliberately rather than leaving nearly invisible text. Eliminate compounded opacity in nested groups.

### 6. [major · rules] RN verification removes mandatory AI provenance

- **Where:** explainer.jsx verificationLabel(true) and the rnVerified rendering branch · confidence high
- **Problem:** The pictured default state has the required AI note, but setting rnVerified=true replaces it with 'RN-verified' and removes both the sparkle label and verify-against-course-materials note. The owner amendment explicitly requires those to remain on all AI content; human verification does not erase AI provenance.
- **Fix:** Always retain the AI-origin label and verification note for AI-generated content. Render RN verification as an additional status, not a replacement.

### 7. [minor · consistency] Restore 'net' in the isotonic explanation

- **Where:** Images 1, 2, 8, 9: isotonic panel; step 'isotonic' caption and narration · confidence high
- **Problem:** Fact 2 correctly says 'no net water shift,' and the arrows show bidirectional exchange. The panel says 'no shift in/out,' while the caption and narration say water does not shift into or out of cells. Dropping 'net' can imply that water exchange stops.
- **Fix:** Use 'no net water shift' consistently. Explain that water continues moving both ways at equal rates, so cell volume remains unchanged.
