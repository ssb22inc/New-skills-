## Findings

### 1. [blocker · clinical] "Only 3% saline" is not valid when glucose remains elevated

- **Where:** Fact 5; DEX[2]; step "dextrose" caption and narration; images 1, 6, 8, 13 · confidence high
- **Problem:** Glucose can remain an effective extracellular osmole when glucose delivery exceeds disposal, particularly with impaired insulin action. Dextrose-containing infusions can therefore sustain hypertonicity while hyperglycemia persists. Saying that uptake may be slower does not justify the later unconditional statement that only 3% saline keeps pulling water out of cells.
- **Fix:** Make the conclusion conditional: "After the dextrose has been taken up and metabolized, the remaining fluid determines the effect; among the fluids shown, 3% NaCl remains hypertonic. If glucose accumulates, dextrose-containing fluids can also increase plasma tonicity." Apply this qualification consistently to the picture text, caption, narration and facts.

### 2. [major · pedagogy] The crystalloid distribution rule is presented as a tonicity rule

- **Where:** Fact 2: capillary passage and roughly one-quarter intravascular retention · confidence high
- **Problem:** Isotonicity describes effects across cell membranes; it does not establish retention across capillary walls. The approximately one-quarter intravascular distribution applies to isotonic crystalloids, not every isotonic IV fluid. Colloids such as 5% albumin distribute differently.
- **Fix:** Scope this passage explicitly to "isotonic crystalloids such as 0.9% NaCl and lactated Ringer's." Keep the approximate distribution separate from the definition of isotonicity.
