## Findings

### 1. [blocker · visual] Slough and animated depth markers sit below the wound

- **Where:** Images 1, 2, 5, 9, 10, 13: Stage 3 slough; source craterPath(), floorOf(), and Deepest · confidence high
- **Problem:** craterPath() uses the intended floor as a quadratic control point, which the curve never reaches. The Stage 3 crater bottoms near y=134, but slough is drawn near y=140, inside intact fat. Animated markers are also misplaced: Stage 4's marker is at y=174, the muscle–bone boundary, although its crater ends near y=156. Static screenshots conceal the marker error because Loop removes it.
- **Fix:** Make the crater actually reach its specified floor, or calculate its true floor from the geometry. Use that same geometry for adherent slough and depth markers. Test live playback as well as paused renders.

### 2. [blocker · clinical] The taught Stage 4 definition omits qualifying findings

- **Where:** Images 6 and 14; step "stage4"; Stage 4 column label; Frame description · confidence high
- **Problem:** Fact 4 correctly includes exposed or directly palpable fascia, muscle, tendon, ligament, cartilage or bone. The caption, narration, diagram label and accessible description narrow this to exposed muscle, tendon or bone. Exposed fascia alone or directly palpable bone still qualifies as Stage 4 but does not fit the definition students are taught.
- **Fix:** Preserve both 'exposed or directly palpable' and the complete qualifying tissue list in the learner-facing explanation and accessible description.

### 3. [blocker · consistency] The standalone diagram loses the head-of-bed safety qualification

- **Where:** Images 1, 8, 9, 16: HOB tile; PREVENT; Frame description versus fact 8 and step "prevent" · confidence high
- **Problem:** The facts and narration say 'unless contraindicated,' but the inline tile and accessible description state HOB ≤30° without qualification. Following that standalone instruction could conflict with respiratory needs or aspiration precautions requiring greater elevation.
- **Fix:** Put 'if clinically appropriate' directly on the tile and preserve the qualification in the accessible description. Explain that respiratory and aspiration-prevention needs may require a higher position.

### 4. [major · pedagogy] Staging is not simply the depth of damage

- **Where:** Images 2 and 10; step "layers" caption and narration; source introductory comments · confidence high
- **Problem:** The explanation reduces staging to a depth ruler. Staging uses tissue findings and the extent of tissue loss, not absolute depth or the full underlying damage. Anatomical thickness varies, and substantial deep tissue damage can exist beneath intact skin.
- **Fix:** Say that stages describe tissue findings and the extent of loss, with depth varying by location. Clarify that the categories are not a required progression and healing injuries are not reverse-staged.

### 5. [major · clinical] Every-two-hour repositioning is presented as a universal requirement

- **Where:** Fact 8; images 1, 8, 9, 16; step "prevent"; PREVENT reposition tile; Frame description · confidence high
- **Problem:** Current pressure-injury prevention guidance individualizes repositioning frequency according to mobility, skin response, clinical condition, comfort and the support surface. 'At least every 2 hours' is a common teaching convention, not a universal minimum interval applicable to every prevention plan.
- **Fix:** Teach an individualized repositioning schedule with reassessment. If retaining q2h for NCLEX context, identify it as a commonly specified schedule rather than an unconditional rule.

### 6. [major · clinical] The Stage 2 versus deep tissue injury distinction is incomplete

- **Where:** Facts 2 and 6; steps "stage2" and "other"; images 4, 7, 12, 15; Frame description · confidence high
- **Problem:** Stage 2 is described as simply 'a blister,' without specifying serum-filled. A pressure-related blood-filled blister can represent deep tissue pressure injury, not Stage 2. Meanwhile, the displayed and spoken DTPI explanation drops the non-blanchable criterion that appears in fact 6, making persistent discoloration alone sound sufficient.
- **Fix:** Specify an intact or ruptured serum-filled blister for Stage 2. Retain persistent non-blanchable discoloration for DTPI and include its blood-filled-blister presentation within a pressure/shear context. State that Stage 2 has no slough or eschar.

### 7. [major · pedagogy] Redness-only teaching can obscure Stage 1 findings in darker skin

- **Where:** Step "stage1"; images 3 and 11; Stage 1 labels and Frame description · confidence high
- **Problem:** Every learner-facing description relies on visible redness without explaining that Stage 1 may appear differently in darkly pigmented skin. A student could incorrectly exclude an injury because obvious red coloration is absent.
- **Fix:** Add that color changes may differ in darker skin and assessment also considers localized temperature, firmness and sensation changes. Do not make visible redness the sole recognition cue.

### 8. [major · consistency] Mentioning a stage is treated as confirming that stage

- **Where:** Source stageFrom() and parameter-driven column highlighting · confidence high
- **Problem:** stageFrom("This is not a stage 4 pressure injury.") returns stage 4. The function also accepts quoted incorrect documentation or a proposed classification as the confirmed stage. Using that result for the matching outline can highlight a stage the question explicitly rejects.
- **Fix:** Use a structured, validated stage associated with the current pressure injury. Do not derive a confirmed classification from an arbitrary stage mention; return null when the mention is negated, hypothetical or otherwise unconfirmed.

### 9. [major · accessibility] Core teaching text becomes roughly 8–10 CSS pixels on a phone

- **Where:** All images: column descriptions, tissue legend and prevention subtitles; SVG T sizes 9.5–12 · confidence high
- **Problem:** The 360-unit SVG scales down inside the padded phone-width container. Its 9.5–12-unit labels therefore render at approximately 8–10 CSS pixels, substantially smaller than the readable caption below. Essential staging and prevention information requires zooming.
- **Fix:** Reflow or stack the content at phone widths and increase essential label sizes. Prefer responsive HTML text where possible rather than shrinking an entire four-column teaching diagram.

### 10. [major · accessibility] The tissue key becomes unreadable during individual staging lessons

- **Where:** Images 3–6 and 11–14: tissue legend; step focus arrays; kit.jsx G · confidence high
- **Problem:** The individual stage steps omit "legend" from their focus arrays, so G reduces the tissue labels to 18% opacity. Their contrast falls far below 4.5:1 in both themes precisely when learners need the key to identify which layer the wound reaches. Other dimmed instructional text has the same contrast failure.
- **Fix:** Keep the legend in focus during every stage lesson. Preserve readable text contrast when de-emphasizing content; use borders, emphasis or genuinely hidden secondary content instead of fading instructional text to 18%.
