## Assessment

Do not publish as-is. The condition matcher can select clinically incorrect precautions. The diagram also has misleading teaching shortcuts, unreadable phone-scale content, and publication states that violate the AI-labeling rules.

## Findings

### 1. [blocker · clinical] Disease-name matching selects incorrect precautions

- **Where:** isolation.jsx — CONDITIONS and precautionFor(); question-specific highlighting branches not exercised by Images 1–14 · confidence high
- **Problem:** The matcher ignores disease form and infectious status. 'Latent tuberculosis' returns airborne, although latent TB does not require airborne isolation. 'Congenital rubella' returns droplet instead of contact. 'Localized varicella-zoster in an immunocompetent client, lesions covered' returns chickenpox with airborne plus contact, although that shingles presentation generally requires standard precautions. Historical or negated diagnoses can also trigger an outline.
- **Fix:** Use structured diagnosis and precaution metadata, or require the relevant clinical qualifiers before highlighting. Return null when context is insufficient. Label the TB example as infectious pulmonary/laryngeal TB, distinguish acquired from congenital rubella, and distinguish chickenpox from localized/disseminated zoster. Add regression tests for these examples.

### 2. [major · pedagogy] Three feet is presented as a transmission boundary

- **Where:** Images 1, 5, 7, 8, 12, 14 — droplet heading and distance graphic; step 'droplet' caption/narration; Frame description · confidence high
- **Problem:** The bounded '3ft' graphic and repeated statement that droplets fall within about three feet suggest a dependable stopping distance. Respiratory droplets can travel farther; three feet is not a reliable boundary for transmission risk. The correct mask-on-entry instruction does not eliminate that misleading mental model.
- **Fix:** Remove the distance bracket and replace the heading/explanation with short-range respiratory spread without a fixed cutoff. Retain surgical-mask-on-entry guidance.

### 3. [major · clinical] A private droplet room is overstated as mandatory

- **Where:** Fact 3; Images 1, 5, 8, 12 — droplet 'Room private'; Frame description · confidence high
- **Problem:** The fact says droplet precautions require a private room, and the picture offers no alternative. A single room is preferred, but appropriate cohorting with the same pathogen is an accepted alternative when unavailable. Contrasting this with 'private or cohort' for contact precautions teaches an incorrect room-assignment distinction.
- **Fix:** State 'single room preferred; cohort with the same pathogen when appropriate under infection-prevention guidance.' Keep the fact, diagram, and accessible description consistent.

### 4. [major · consistency] The static reference omits airborne transport masking

- **Where:** Images 1 and 8 — inline airborne row versus Fact 4 and step 'transport' · confidence high
- **Problem:** Only the droplet row says the client masks for transport. The airborne row omits this action, and its only mask cue is the staff N95. Students viewing the inline diagram alone can incorrectly associate client transport masking only with droplet precautions.
- **Fix:** Add a shared, visible transport annotation for droplet AND airborne precautions. Explicitly distinguish the client's surgical mask from the staff respirator.

### 5. [major · accessibility] Core clinical instructions shrink to roughly 9–10 px on phones

- **Where:** Images 1–14 — SVG clinical labels; Row text sizes and fixed 360-unit canvas · confidence high
- **Problem:** Most example lists, PPE instructions, and room requirements use 10.5–11-unit text. After phone-width container padding and SVG scaling, this is roughly 9–10 CSS px. The distance and respirator labels are smaller still. These labels also ignore the app's --read-size setting.
- **Fix:** Reflow the content into taller, stacked cards or responsive text outside the SVG. Keep clinical body text approximately 14–16 CSS px at phone width and make it respond to the app's reading-size setting.

### 6. [major · accessibility] Dimming makes reference text fail contrast requirements

- **Where:** Images 2–7 and 9–14 — nonfocused rows; kit.jsx G() opacity 0.18 · confidence high
- **Problem:** Applying 18% opacity to whole groups makes their clinical text nearly disappear in both themes, far below 4.5:1. These are informational reference rows, not disabled controls; retaining them as unreadable context does not satisfy the contrast rule.
- **Fix:** Keep informative text at compliant contrast. Emphasize the current row with borders, weight, or background treatment instead of reducing the entire group's opacity.

### 7. [major · accessibility] Step buttons have undersized touch targets

- **Where:** Images 2–7 and 9–14 — step navigation dots; DIAGRAM_CSS .dg-dot · confidence high
- **Problem:** Each step button is only 10 × 10 CSS px with 6 px gaps. The neighboring targets are too close to satisfy the spacing alternative to the 24 × 24 CSS-px minimum, making phone navigation difficult for users with limited dexterity.
- **Fix:** Give each button at least a 24 × 24 CSS-px hit area while retaining a smaller visible dot inside it. Wrap or rearrange the controls if necessary.

### 8. [major · rules] Correct precaution matches use the error/critical color

- **Where:** isolation.jsx — Row highlight rectangle when params.types matches a condition · confidence high
- **Problem:** Every condition-matched row receives a coral outline, including routine matches such as lice or influenza. This uses coral as a generic selection color rather than for an incorrect or critical state, conflicting with the project's color semantics.
- **Fix:** Use a teal outline and an explicit 'Required precautions' label for matched rows. Reserve coral for actual errors or critical warnings.

### 9. [major · rules] RN verification removes mandatory AI provenance

- **Where:** explainer.jsx — verificationLabel(true) and the dg-ai rendering branch · confidence high
- **Problem:** When rnVerified is true, the component replaces the sparkle AI label and verify-against-your-materials note with only 'RN-verified.' The owner amendment requires those disclosures to remain on all AI content, including approved content.
- **Fix:** Always retain the sparkle AI disclosure and verification note. Render RN verification as an additional badge, not as a replacement.
