## Assessment

Do not publish. The condition-matching logic can select droplet precautions for a client with measles and falsely assign isolation to historical, ruled-out, or misattributed conditions. These failures are not visible in the supplied screenshots.

## Findings

### 1. [blocker · clinical] A negative rubella test can downgrade measles to droplet precautions

- **Where:** isolation.jsx — currentMatch(), NOT_ASSERTED_AFTER, precautionFor() · confidence high
- **Problem:** For "A client has measles and a negative rubella test.", the function returns rubella/droplet. The word "negative" after measles suppresses the measles match, although it belongs to the rubella test. Rubella then counts as an infection because "a negative" is not recognized before its name. The resulting picture incorrectly labels droplet precautions as sufficient for this client.
- **Fix:** Bind test results and negation to their specific condition, rather than searching a character window around each disease name. Add this exact regression case; its result must be measles/airborne.

### 2. [blocker · clinical] Historical and explicitly negated TB can count as current disease

- **Where:** isolation.jsx — currentMatch(), NOT_ASSERTED_BEFORE and NOT_ASSERTED_AFTER · confidence high
- **Problem:** "Pulmonary TB is not suspected in this client." returns airborne precautions because post-mention "is not suspected" is not recognized. "The client completed treatment for pulmonary TB five years ago and has no active disease." also returns airborne. The matcher therefore turns explicit non-current disease into a current client requirement.
- **Fix:** Require an affirmative, current or suspected infection assertion. Handle post-mention negation and completed historical treatment, or abstain when those constructions cannot be resolved. Add both regression cases.

### 3. [blocker · clinical] Zoster can still borrow another person's immune status

- **Where:** isolation.jsx — CLIENT_IMMUNOCOMPROMISED and the generic zoster entry · confidence high
- **Problem:** "An immunocompetent client has localized zoster with covered lesions and an immunocompromised child at home." returns airborne plus contact. The regex finds "immunocompromised child" in the same sentence and assigns that status to the client. Under the diagram's own stated rule, this client's covered localized zoster requires standard precautions.
- **Fix:** Attach immune status to the person who has zoster, not any client/patient/child noun in the sentence. Use structured patient attributes or abstain when subject attribution is uncertain.

### 4. [blocker · clinical] A ruled-out disseminated-zoster diagnosis does not end the special branch

- **Where:** isolation.jsx — generic zoster entry, sentence.unless · confidence high
- **Problem:** "An immunocompromised client has localized zoster with covered lesions; disseminated zoster has been ruled out." returns airborne plus contact. The disseminated entry is correctly rejected, but the generic entry only recognizes wording such as "dissemination has been ruled out", not the equivalent ruled-out diagnosis. This contradicts fact 6.
- **Fix:** Recognize exclusion of disseminated zoster as exclusion of dissemination for that client. Evaluate the disease form and its exclusion together before selecting precautions; add this exact regression case.

### 5. [blocker · clinical] Discontinuation criteria are ignored except for head lice

- **Where:** isolation.jsx — CONDITIONS and precautionFor(), treatment/discontinuation handling · confidence high
- **Problem:** "A client with meningococcal disease has received effective antibiotics for 48 hours." still returns droplet, although droplet precautions ordinarily end after 24 hours of effective therapy. "A client with chickenpox has all lesions dry and crusted." still returns airborne plus contact despite meeting the usual discontinuation criterion. These are clinically wrong client-specific requirements, not merely missing details in a generic disease list.
- **Fix:** Evaluate supported pathogen-specific discontinuation criteria. When treatment or resolution details are present but cannot be interpreted reliably, return null rather than asserting that transmission-based precautions remain required.

### 6. [major · consistency] Comparison questions can receive a false client-specific highlight

- **Where:** isolation.jsx — precautionFor(), byGroup deduplication and final selection · confidence high
- **Problem:** "Which client needs isolation: one with latent TB or one with pulmonary TB?" returns airborne and displays "Needed for this client". There is no single asserted client condition. This violates the function's stated abstention rule for comparison questions; grouping disease variants does not resolve which client is being discussed.
- **Fix:** Detect multiple subjects and comparison/question-option structures before selecting a condition. Do not emit a client-specific requirement unless one current condition is unambiguously attached to the target client.

### 7. [minor · pedagogy] Contact cohorting never specifies the same pathogen

- **Where:** Images 1, 3, 4, 8, 10 and 11 — Contact Room line; step contact caption/narration; fact 2 · confidence high
- **Problem:** The contact material says only "single room or cohort", while the droplet material explicitly requires the same pathogen. A novice could interpret the contact row as permitting different contact-isolation infections to share a room.
- **Fix:** State "cohort with clients who have the same pathogen and compatible additional precautions" consistently in the contact row, caption, narration and facts.
