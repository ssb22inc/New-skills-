Needs revision before publication. Step cropping visibly cuts labels, patient-match highlighting competes with the active lesson, and the hyperkalemia priority wording teaches an unjustified report-first rule.

## Findings

### 1. [major · visual] Step viewports leave chopped neighboring labels

- **Where:** Images 2–6 and 9–13; kit.jsx viewFor() and potassium.jsx regions · confidence high
- **Problem:** The range step ends with a sliced hypokalemia heading. ECG steps show clipped gauge labels or fragments of the neighboring ECG section. The care steps also retain an entire unrelated panel because viewFor() takes one continuous span between separated focus regions. These are visible rendering defects in both themes.
- **Fix:** Exclude unrelated groups from each step’s rendered viewport rather than cropping indiscriminately through the full drawing. Set viewport boundaries outside complete text bounds; use a step-specific layout for separated focus panels.

### 2. [major · visual] Hyperkalemia remains prominently outlined during hypokalemia teaching

- **Where:** Images 3, 4, 10 and 11; potassium.jsx match group · confidence high
- **Problem:** The patient-match rectangle renders on every step, and dg-solid prevents it from dimming. During hypo-care, the hyperkalemia panel therefore retains the strongest outline while the lesson concerns low potassium. The outline has no label distinguishing patient classification from instructional focus.
- **Fix:** Show the patient-match outline only in the worked step and, if needed, the inline overview. Keep instructional focus separate from patient classification.

### 3. [major · pedagogy] “First recognized and reported” creates a misleading priority rule

- **Where:** Step hyper-care caption and narration; images 6 and 13 · confidence medium
- **Problem:** The explicit word “first,” followed by “NCLEX priority reasoning,” encourages students to select notification before considering immediate client assessment, cardiac monitoring or emergency escalation. The correct priority depends on the scenario; an unstable client or dangerous rhythm cannot be reduced to a report-first sequence.
- **Fix:** Remove the universal first-action claim. Explain that clinical assessment, cardiac evaluation and prompt escalation are prioritized according to the scenario and may occur concurrently.

### 4. [minor · consistency] “Then removed” reintroduces the serial sequence the diagram disclaims

- **Where:** Step hyper-care caption and narration · confidence high
- **Problem:** Both versions say the goals can overlap, but subsequently say potassium is “then removed.” That wording can suggest waiting until shifting treatment is finished before arranging elimination.
- **Fix:** Replace “then removed” with wording that identifies removal as another goal pursued alongside temporizing measures when indicated.

### 5. [minor · consistency] The formatter can still display an abnormal value as a boundary value

- **Where:** kit.jsx exact(); potassium.jsx displayK() and dynamicCaption() · confidence high
- **Problem:** exact() accepts differences smaller than 1e-12 instead of requiring a numeric round trip. For example, k=3.4999999999995 is classified as hypokalemia but displayed as 3.5; k=5.0000000000005 is classified as hyperkalemia but displayed as 5.0. These are extreme-precision edge cases, but they contradict the stated guarantee for every supplied value.
- **Fix:** Require Number(v.toFixed(d)) === v, or preserve the numeric round-trip string and add only necessary trailing zeros. Add tests immediately above and below both boundaries at near-machine precision.
