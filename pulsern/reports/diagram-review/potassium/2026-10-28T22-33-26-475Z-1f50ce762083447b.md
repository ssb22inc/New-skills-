## Assessment

Do not publish as-is. The main risks are ECG-based false reassurance, an overgeneralized emergency sequence, and boundary values whose displayed numbers contradict their classification. Mobile readability and several shared-player behaviors also fail review. Actual narration recordings and the app-wide disclaimer/footer were not available to verify.

## Findings

### 1. [blocker · clinical] ECG changes are presented as a predictable progression and an urgency gate

- **Where:** Steps hyper-ecg and worked; images 5, 7, 12, 14; potassium.dynamicCaption() hyperkalemia branch · confidence high
- **Problem:** Peaked T waves do not reliably appear first, and ECG findings do not track serum potassium predictably. Severe hyperkalemia can have a normal or minimally changed ECG. Every elevated value, including k=8.0, gets the same 'watch the monitor ... if the ECG changes' caption. This can teach students to wait for ECG changes before recognizing an emergency.
- **Fix:** Describe these as possible classic findings, not a guaranteed sequence. State that a normal ECG does not exclude dangerous hyperkalemia. Add reference-backed urgency handling for severely elevated values rather than reusing the observation-oriented caption. Label the outlined strip as illustrative, not the client's established ECG.

### 2. [blocker · clinical] The emergency mnemonic becomes an unconditional serial protocol

- **Where:** Images 1, 6, 8, 13; hyper-act Emergency order; hyper-care caption/narration; Frame description · confidence high
- **Problem:** Fact 9 supplies an ECG-change indication for calcium, but the panel, narration and accessible description omit it. 'The order matters ... Give calcium ... Then insulin ... Then remove it' also implies that potassium removal waits until earlier stages are completed. This is not a universal treatment sequence. The removal list further makes delayed-action binders, renal-dependent diuresis and dialysis look interchangeable.
- **Fix:** Restore the indication for cardiac stabilization. Present stabilization, temporary intracellular shifting and definitive removal as treatment goals whose implementation can overlap under the applicable protocol. Clarify that removal depends on urgency and renal function; binders are not sole rescue therapy for life-threatening hyperkalemia, and diuretics require sufficient renal response.

### 3. [blocker · consistency] Rounding produces clinically contradictory boundary examples

- **Where:** potassium.dynamicCaption(); PotassiumDiagram Gauge decimals={1}; classifyPotassium() · confidence high
- **Problem:** Classification uses the unrounded input, while the gauge and caption display one decimal. For k=3.49, the student sees '3.5 ... below 3.5, so hypokalemia.' For k=5.01, the student sees '5.0 ... above 5.0, so hyperkalemia.' The abnormal marker and strip reinforce a classification that contradicts the displayed value.
- **Fix:** Preserve enough input precision to keep the displayed value consistent with classification. Do not silently round an abnormal result onto an inclusive normal boundary. Test 3.49, 3.5, 5.0 and 5.01 across classification, caption, marker and highlighting.

### 4. [major · consistency] The spoken cause list drops the diuretic class distinction

- **Where:** Step hypo-care caption/narration; images 4 and 11 · confidence high
- **Problem:** The facts and picture correctly specify loop/thiazide diuretics, but the caption and narration say only 'diuretics.' The same diagram identifies potassium-sparing diuretics as a cause of hyperkalemia. The unqualified spoken rule invites the opposite medication association.
- **Fix:** Say 'loop and thiazide diuretics' in both caption and narration.

### 6. [major · accessibility] Core diagram text renders at roughly 8–10 CSS pixels on a phone

- **Where:** All images; Bullets, Stepper, Callout and Gauge text · confidence high
- **Problem:** The SVG uses 9.5–11.5-unit text and then scales the entire canvas into a padded mobile card. The emergency explanations are especially small. The larger reading-size setting affects captions, not SVG labels, so it does not fix the teaching content.
- **Fix:** Reflow the care panels into responsive text or split the diagram into readable sections. Use phone-sized body text rather than shrinking the whole illustration; verify at actual CSS width, not enlarged screenshot resolution.

### 7. [major · accessibility] Text contrast fails the project's minimum

- **Where:** Light images 1, 3–6: coral labels and Emergency order; stepped views in both themes; C.coral and G opacity · confidence high
- **Problem:** Light-theme coral #D9503F has approximately 4.1:1 contrast on white and 3.9:1 on the care-panel surface, below 4.5:1 for these small labels. Separately, forcing unfocused text to 18% opacity makes retained reference content unreadable in both themes.
- **Fix:** Use a contrast-compliant danger text token for small coral labels. De-emphasize panel backgrounds or borders instead of reducing retained text below the required contrast. Test composited colors, including focus states.

### 8. [major · accessibility] Space overrides native navigation-button activation

- **Where:** src/explainer.jsx: onKey handler on the section · confidence high
- **Problem:** When a previous, next or step-dot button has focus, its Space keydown bubbles to the section. The handler prevents the button's normal activation and toggles playback instead. Keyboard users cannot rely on standard button behavior.
- **Fix:** Do not intercept Space from interactive descendants. Preserve native button activation and scope playback shortcuts to an appropriate noninteractive player target.

### 9. [major · accessibility] Step buttons have undersized touch targets

- **Fix:** Provide at least 24×24-pixel nonoverlapping targets, preferably larger for touch, while keeping the visible dot small. Reflow the controls if necessary.

### 13. [minor · visual] The U-wave callout uses the wrong beat spacing
